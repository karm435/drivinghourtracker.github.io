Privacy policy

Effective 21 October 2026

Driving Hour Tracker doesn't collect your personal information. There are no accounts, ads, analytics or tracking. Your logbook lives on your phone. On iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch it also syncs through your own iCloud account when you're signed in; on Android and Wear OS it stays on the device, along with any backup files you choose to save.

This policy covers the Driving Hour Tracker app for iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch, the Driving Hour Tracker app for Android and Wear OS, and this website. Driving Hour Tracker is published by KarmaAcademy in Australia. "We" and "us" in this policy mean KarmaAcademy. Where the two apps differ, the section says which one it applies to.

What the app stores

On iPhone and Android alike, the app keeps your logbook in its own database on your device:

  • The learner's name, the names of supervisors, and your total and night hour goals.
  • Each drive: its date, start and finish times, day and night minutes, supervisor, notes, any extra fields you add, whether it was recorded or added by hand, and whether it needs review.
  • The route of a recorded drive, as a list of location points, and the distance worked out from it.
  • While a drive is being recorded, a journal of its progress, written every 30 seconds so the drive survives if the app closes.
  • Settings, such as the logbook you have open, whether driving suggestions are on and, on Android, the car you chose for Start from car Bluetooth.

We never receive any of this. Deleting a drive removes it, removing a route deletes its location points without changing the drive's hours, and deleting the app removes everything stored on that device.

iCloud iPhone and iPad

If you're signed in to iCloud on your iPhone or iPad, the app syncs your logbook to your own iCloud account using Apple's CloudKit, so the same logbook appears on both. This is between your devices and Apple. We have no access to your iCloud account or its contents, and the app works without iCloud; the logbook then stays on the device alone.

iCloud is covered by Apple's privacy policy. You can turn iCloud off for the app in the Settings app under your Apple Account, then iCloud.

On Android there is no iCloud and no sync. The logbook stays on the phone; to move it, save a backup and restore it on the other device.

Sharing a logbook iPhone and iPad

Sharing is optional. If you invite someone to a logbook, they receive a live copy of the whole logbook through iCloud, including routes and notes, either to view or to add and edit drives. They need an iCloud account and the app. The owner of a logbook decides who has access and can remove anyone at any time; someone who leaves a shared logbook loses their copy. Adding a supervisor's name in Settings on its own shares nothing: it's only a name recorded on your drives.

On Android sharing isn't available, and the Android app can't open an invitation. To show a supervisor your hours, give them a PDF report or a backup file.

Location

The app asks for location access only when you choose to record routes or, on iPhone, turn on driving suggestions. You can record drives without it; the timer and the logbook don't need location.

On iPhone and iPad

  • While using the app: records the route of the current drive and works out, from sunset and sunrise along that route, how much of the drive counts as night.
  • Always: additionally lets the app notice when you start driving so it can suggest recording, and keep recording the route when the app is in the background.

On Android

The app asks for precise location when you start a drive with Record your route on. It uses location only while that drive is recording, with the recording notification showing, to record the route and work out night time from sunset and sunrise along it. The app never asks for background location ("Allow all the time"), and uses no location between drives. Driving suggestions on Android come from physical activity, not location.

On both platforms, location points are stored only in the route of that drive, on your device, and on iPhone in your iCloud account if iCloud is on. Night time is calculated on the device. Routes are left out of PDF reports and spreadsheets. You can remove a route from any drive, and change location access at any time: in the Settings app under Driving Hour Tracker on iPhone, or under the app's permissions in Android settings.

Motion and physical activity

With your permission, the app uses your phone's motion activity to tell when you're driving, so it can suggest starting a drive and offer to finish one after you park. This happens on the device. The app doesn't store motion data; only the drive's start and finish times are kept, like any other drive.

  • On iPhone this is the Motion and Fitness permission.
  • On Android it is the Physical activity permission. The app receives only the kind of activity Android detects, such as in a vehicle or still, never step counts or health data.

Notifications

Notifications such as "Starting a practice drive?" and, on Android, the timer that shows while a drive is recording are created on your device by the app. There is no notification server and nothing is sent to us. On Android the app asks for the Notifications permission the first time you start a drive; without it, recording still works but the timer notification stays hidden.

Maps

Routes are drawn on a map inside the app: Apple Maps on iPhone and iPad, Google Maps on Android. To draw the map, the map provider's software receives the map region you're viewing, together with the standard device information such requests carry. The route's points are drawn by the app on the device and aren't sent to the provider by the app. Map traffic on iPhone is covered by Apple's privacy policy, and on Android by Google's privacy policy. A drive without a route shows no map at all.

Bluetooth and your car Android

If you turn on Start from car Bluetooth, Android's companion device pairing lets you choose your car once. From then on the app is told when that one device connects and disconnects, and it keeps the car's name so Settings can show which car you chose. The app doesn't scan for other devices, read anything from your car, or use Bluetooth for anything else. Forget this car in Settings removes the pairing.

Siri, Shortcuts, widgets and Apple Watch iPhone

Siri requests, Shortcuts, the Control Center toggle, widgets and the Live Activity are handled by iOS on your device. The app shares a small summary of your logbook, such as your hours and the current drive, with its own widgets and with the Apple Watch app on your paired watch. That summary stays within your devices.

Widgets, Quick Settings, shortcuts and Wear OS Android

The home-screen widget, the Quick Settings tile and the app shortcuts read a small summary of your logbook that the app keeps on the phone: your hours, your goals and the current drive. The Wear OS app receives a copy of that summary over the link between your phone and watch, and sends back the commands you tap, such as start, pause and finish. That link is provided by Google Play services under Google's privacy policy; the watch never holds the full logbook, and we never receive the summary.

Files you export

PDF reports, spreadsheets, backups and recovery files are created on your device only when you ask for them, and go wherever you choose to save or share them. A backup is a file you keep yourself: it contains the whole logbook, including routes, and restoring it on another phone, iPhone or Android, is how a logbook moves between devices. We don't receive these files.

This website

This website is a set of static pages. It sets no cookies, runs no analytics, and loads nothing from third parties: no fonts, scripts or trackers. The App Store and Google Play badges are images served from this site, and following one takes you to that store under its own privacy policy. The service that hosts the site may keep standard server logs, such as the address and time of a request, in order to deliver the pages.

Young people

Learner drivers are often under 18. The app is designed so it collects no personal information from anyone, of any age, and has no accounts or messaging.
